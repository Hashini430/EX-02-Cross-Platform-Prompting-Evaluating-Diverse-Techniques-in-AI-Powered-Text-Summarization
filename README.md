# 1. Introduction

Technical articles often contain specialized terminology and complex concepts that can be difficult for undergraduate students to understand quickly. Summarization helps reduce a long article to its essential ideas while preserving the original meaning.

Generative AI platforms can summarize technical documents using natural-language instructions. However, different platforms and prompts may produce different results. A summary may be clear but incomplete, concise but inaccurate, or fluent while introducing unsupported information.

Therefore, selecting an appropriate AI platform and prompting technique requires a systematic evaluation based on measurable criteria rather than assumptions.

## 1.1 Problem Statement

The main problem is the absence of a controlled comparison establishing which combination of AI platform and prompting technique produces the most suitable technical summaries for undergraduate students.

A fair comparison requires:

- The same source article for every experiment.
- Consistent summary-length requirements.
- Clearly defined prompting techniques.
- Standardized evaluation criteria.
- Repeated trials.
- Recorded response times and quality scores.

## 1.2 Scope

This study focuses on summarizing an approximately 500-word technical article titled **"The Basics of Blockchain Technology"** into a summary of approximately 150 words.

The results should be interpreted within this experimental context and not automatically generalized to all technical subjects.

# 3. Background

## 3.1 AI-Based Text Summarization

Text summarization can be divided into two major approaches.

- **Extractive summarization:** Selects important sentences or phrases from the original document.
- **Abstractive summarization:** Generates new wording that conveys the main ideas of the source.

Generative AI commonly performs abstractive summarization. Although this can improve readability, it may introduce factual errors, omit essential qualifications, or add claims that are not supported by the source.

Consequently, summary evaluation must consider both readability and factual faithfulness.

## 3.2 Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering involves designing instructions and providing context to guide an AI model toward a desired output.

The five prompting techniques examined in this study are:

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| Zero-shot prompting | Gives an instruction without a worked example. |
| Few-shot prompting | Provides an example of the expected output style. |
| Structured checklist prompting | Specifies steps for selecting and checking important information. |
| Role-based prompting | Assigns a role, such as university lecturer or educational content curator. |
| Constraint-based prompting | Defines explicit requirements for length, audience, structure, and content. |

These techniques may overlap. To improve fairness, the source article, target length, and core accuracy requirements should remain consistent across experiments.

## 4.2 Illustrative Article: The Basics of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain is a type of distributed ledger that records transactions across a network of computers. Instead of relying on one central database, participating computers maintain copies of a shared record. The ledger is organized into blocks, and each block contains a group of transactions and information linking it to the previous block.

A cryptographic hash is a fixed-length value generated from input data. Even a small change to the input normally produces a different hash, making hashes useful for detecting changes. In a blockchain, blocks commonly include the hash of the preceding block. Altering an earlier record therefore changes its hash and can break the links to later blocks. This structure helps make records tamper-evident, although the level of protection depends on the network's design and security.

Because several participants maintain the ledger, the network needs a way to agree on which transactions and blocks should be accepted. This process is called consensus. Proof of Work requires participants to perform computational work to propose blocks. Proof of Stake selects or assigns block proposal and validation responsibilities based on participants' stake under a protocol's rules. These mechanisms have different security, performance, and resource trade-offs.

Blockchain can provide transparency when records are visible to network participants, but not every blockchain is fully public. Some are permissioned, meaning that access is restricted. Likewise, blockchain records are often difficult to alter, but the technology does not automatically guarantee that the original information entered was correct. Privacy, governance, scalability, energy use, and transaction costs remain important considerations.

Applications include digital assets, supply-chain tracking, shared recordkeeping, and systems that need participants to coordinate without relying entirely on a single administrator. However, blockchain is not the best solution for every database problem. Its suitability depends on whether multiple parties need a shared record, how much trust exists between them, the required transaction rate, and the costs of operating the network.

# 7. Evaluation Framework

Each criterion is scored on a scale from **1 to 5**, where 1 represents poor performance and 5 represents excellent performance.

## 7.1 Accuracy — 35%

Evaluate factual correctness, faithfulness to the source, coverage of essential concepts, and absence of unsupported claims.

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Major factual errors or unsupported claims |
| 2 | Several errors or important omissions |
| 3 | Generally correct but with noticeable omissions |
| 4 | Accurate with only minor omissions |
| 5 | Highly faithful, correct, and sufficiently comprehensive |

## 7.2 Coherence — 20%

Evaluate logical organization, transitions, relationships between concepts, and consistency.

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Disorganized and difficult to follow |
| 2 | Weak structure and frequent disconnections |
| 3 | Understandable but unevenly organized |
| 4 | Clear and logically connected |
| 5 | Exceptionally well-structured and cohesive |

## 7.3 Simplicity — 20%

Evaluate readability for undergraduate students, sentence length, explanations of technical terms, and unnecessary jargon.

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Highly technical and difficult to understand |
| 2 | Frequently unclear |
| 3 | Moderately accessible |
| 4 | Clear and easy to understand |
| 5 | Very accessible without sacrificing accuracy |

## 7.4 Speed — 10%

Measure actual response time in seconds. Report the median, minimum, and maximum across repeated runs.

An optional normalized speed score is:

\[
S_{\text{speed}}=\min\left(5,\frac{5T_{\min}}{T_{\text{median}}}\right)
\]

Where:

- \(T_{\min}\) is the fastest observed median response time among all tested combinations.
- \(T_{\text{median}}\) is the median response time of the evaluated combination.

This score is relative to the experiment and is not a universal measure of platform speed. Do not replace observed times with estimates.

## 7.5 User Experience — 15%

Evaluate ease of prompt submission, output retrieval, formatting, consistency, ease of refinement, and suitability for educational content curation.

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Very difficult or frustrating to use |
| 2 | Several workflow problems |
| 3 | Acceptable with some limitations |
| 4 | Smooth and practical |
| 5 | Highly convenient and suitable for the task |

User experience should be assessed through direct observation or an evaluator survey, rather than inferred solely from summary quality.

## 7.6 Weighted Scoring Formula

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Accuracy | 35% |
| Coherence | 20% |
| Simplicity | 20% |
| Speed | 10% |
| User Experience | 15% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

The overall score is calculated as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Overall Score}={}&0.35A+0.20C+0.20S\\
&+0.10V+0.15U
\end{aligned}
\]

Where:

- \(A\) = Accuracy score
- \(C\) = Coherence score
- \(S\) = Simplicity score
- \(V\) = Normalized speed score
- \(U\) = User-experience score

All components must be on a 1–5 scale. Report the final result to two decimal places.

Accuracy receives the highest weight because misleading educational content is unacceptable.

## 10.1 Accuracy and Source Faithfulness

Accuracy should be evaluated against the supplied article. A technically plausible statement may still be unsupported by the source and therefore inappropriate in a source-faithful summary.

Evaluators should distinguish harmless paraphrasing from unsupported additions, misleading simplifications, and contradictions.

## 10.2 Effects of Prompting Techniques

Zero-shot prompting is straightforward and easy to reproduce. Few-shot prompting provides an example of the desired style. Structured checklists make quality-control requirements explicit, role-based prompting specifies an audience perspective, and constraint-based prompting establishes clear length and format requirements.

Their relative effectiveness must be established from the collected data.

## 10.3 Speed and User Experience

A fast response is useful, but speed alone should not determine the winner. The scoring system assigns 10% to speed and 15% to user experience. Response time must be measured consistently, and usability must be assessed directly.

# 11. Final Recommendation

A final winner cannot be responsibly identified until the required platform-technique combinations have been tested.

The recommended workflow should prioritize source-faithful summaries, clear audience requirements, consistent evaluation, and verification against the original article.

# 12. Limitations

The study has several limitations:

- The original article was not supplied, so an illustrative article is used.
- Testing a single short article cannot represent every technical subject or document length.
- Generative AI outputs may vary between runs.
- Platform versions, interfaces, and access conditions may differ.
- Simplicity and user experience involve some subjective judgment.
- Prompt wording and evaluator expectations may influence results.
- Findings cannot be generalized to every summarization task.
- A cross-platform winner cannot be claimed until actual tests are conducted.

# 13. Conclusion

Comparing AI platforms and prompting techniques for technical summarization requires a controlled and transparent evaluation. The same source article, target length, scoring rubric, and timing method should be used across conditions. Accuracy and source faithfulness are especially important in undergraduate education because a fluent summary can still contain misleading claims.

Zero-shot, few-shot, structured checklist, role-based, and constraint-based prompting provide different ways to guide the model. Their relative effectiveness must be established through repeated testing rather than assumption.

The weighted scoring framework provides a practical method for comparing quality, speed, and user experience. The final recommendation should be based on observed evidence, with experimental limitations clearly documented. Until the required experiments are completed, declaring a definitive winning platform or prompting technique would be unsupported.
